Cumulative Accessibility Risk Simulator

An interactive, step-by-step journey model of a critical public service: Applying for an Emergency Heating Assistance Benefit on a state/provincial portal. 30 discrete interactions, 4 sequential stages, one citizen, one 99 % accessible build.

  • Microsoft Persona Spectrum
  • WCAG 2.2

The proposition under test

A monitoring dashboard reports the portal is 99 % accessible. Every page tripod check for Lighthouse and axe-core returns a perfect 100. The press release says the service now "meets WCAG 2.2 AA."

The question this simulator answers: what is the probability that a single citizen completes the whole 30-step task — not one page, the entire journey?

Because a journey is a serial system, component reliability multiplies. A per-step pass rate of p = 0.99 yields 0.99³⁰ ≈ 73.97 % end-to-end completion. Hiding inside that headline percentage is a concrete injury to at least one person.

Run state — the citizen's finite capacity

Spoon Theory: a citizen has a finite pool of capacity for one journey. Each barrier draws it down. Nothing "fails" them — they run out, and then they stop, which is a different ending. The pool covers this form, not a whole day.

Four ways a defect can treat a citizen

  • TERMINAL Pass or fail. One roll, the journey is over. Example: a drag-only upload with no keyboard path.
  • SPOON COST No wall at all. Each encounter drains a finite energy pool. Enough of them and the citizen gives up — an ending no conformance report records.
  • EFFORT Recoverable, at a price: extra attempts, minutes, and confidence spent on working around the service instead of using it.
  • FRICTION A genuine violation that stops nobody. Real, reportable, and not the reason anyone fails.

Five places the obligation comes from

  • WCAG 2.2 A success criterion is failed.
  • ACT RULE The standardised test surface — and where it silently passes.
  • FPC Section 508 / EN 301 549 functional performance criteria, which describe the citizen rather than the code.
  • SYSTEMIC A barrier entirely outside WCAG 2.2 — a policy, channel, device, language, or identity-proofing exclusion. No SC is violated and the citizen is still stopped.
  • LAW / POLICY A statutory or business rule with no rendering requirement attached.

How the persona-weighted probability is calculated
  1. Component model. Baseline defect rate p = 0.01 (99 % component-level accessibility).
  2. Defect clustering. Accessibility defects are not uniformly distributed. Automated tooling concentrates on the ~30 % of WCAG 2.2 A/AA criteria it can decide; defects concentrate in semantic structure, keyboard/focus management, and pointer interactions.
  3. Conditional exposure. For persona i with sensitivity Ri to the affected criterion families:
    pi = min(0.14, p × Ri)
  4. Multi-domain compounding. A persona constrained in k domains carries pi = min(0.14, 0.002 × ΣRd).
  5. Journey model. P(failure) = 1 − (1 − pi)ⁿ — mathematically identical to hardware serial/availability reliability.
  6. Bound. Defects that are not merely friction but true task blockers (e.g. no keyboard path to a control) have pi = 1.00. For those, no probability reduces: the journey ends at that step, deterministically.

Ri denotes relative exposure to the defect families that matter for that domain — it is an explanatory weighting for the reliability curve, not a per-person clinical prediction.

3. The defensible calculation — population-level, deterministic

Everything above this point depends on how a persona is drawn. That is fine for illustration and useless for argument. This section contains no random draw at all: every figure is either a published statistic or a labelled assumption, and re-running it gives the same answer. If you disagree with the result, the disagreement belongs at the inputs — which are stated.

Three severities, not one

"Accessibility error" is not a single thing. The model separates three outcomes, because they have nothing in common mathematically:

  • TOTAL BLOCKER No path exists. Removes 100 % of the affected Functional Performance Criteria group from the journey. Example: drag-only upload with no keyboard alternative, where SC 2.5.7 has no published ACT rule.
  • PARTIAL BLOCKER A path exists for part of the group. Removes the share with no alternative route — a figure taken from survey data where one exists, and labelled as an assumption where none does. Example: pointer-only date-picker, which excludes the 60.4 % of low-vision respondents who navigate by keyboard.
  • DEGRADER Blocks no one. Raises error and abandonment rate, which compounds over 30 steps. Example: low contrast, the single most-reported failure on the web.

There are no published numbers for partial-blocker fractions. This is a genuine gap in the evidence base, not an oversight here. No standards body publishes the share of a disability group that has no alternative path to a given widget. Every such figure in this model is therefore stated as an explicit assumption with a range, is listed in the assumptions table below, and is marked assumption in the defect record. The total blockers need no such assumption: the absence of a path is observable.

    8. Why automated scanners report green

    Deque's own published position, and the U.S. General Services Administration's testing guidance, both state that automated testing detects roughly 30–40 % of WCAG 2.2 A/AA success criteria (GSA's figure is closer to 30 %; Deque reports ~40 % of issues). The residual 60 %+ requires human judgment and assistive technology. The average vendor audit in the WebAIM Million corpus of the top one million home pages (2025) still finds 51 accessibility errors per home page, despite years of automated monitoring.

    Every defect in this run's ledger is one a crawler cannot decide. The scanner tests the DOM at load. It does not test the journey through time.

    9. Executive summary

    Method notes, sources, and limits

    What the mathematical model does and does not claim
    • The product-of-reliabilities result — 1 − (1−p)ⁿ — follows directly from treating independent steps as a serial system. It is exact arithmetic, not a heuristic.
    • Step independence is a modelling simplification for explanatory purposes. Real automated tests are strongly correlated: a widget library that ships one broken pattern breaks every instance of it. Correlation narrows the variance around the mean; it does not eliminate the expected loss of task completion.
    • The recovery term. Because defects are not uniformly fatal, the live run separates two kinds: friction defects (which the citizen may work around, at cost) and deterministic blockers (which end the task). Only blockers stop the run. This prevents the simulator from overstating the loss and keeps the arithmetic honest.
    • Ri weightings are illustrative. They express relative exposure by capability domain and should be calibrated with real task-completion data before being quoted as a forecast for any specific population.
    • The 99 % baseline is a stipulation, generously granted for the sake of argument. Published government benchmarking for complex multi-step services sits far below it.

    Scope and disclaimer

    This artefact is a teaching and advocacy model, not a conformance claim, not a legal opinion, and not a substitute for manual testing with assistive technology. It is designed to be quoted carefully: quote the arithmetic and the standards citations, not the illustrative weightings, which require calibration against real completion data.

    Single-file artefact. No network requests, no external fonts, no tracking. Keyboard-operable end to end; status changes are announced through ARIA live regions; charts have data-table equivalents; motion is limited to a status pulse.
